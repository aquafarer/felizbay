Townhome owners and urban dwellers are told to want "compact water features" — usually a stock tank or a kiddie pool that neither looks like nor works like a pool. The practical alternative is a true drop-stitch pool in its smaller sizes: rigid, straight-walled, genuinely deep enough to soak in, and gone without a trace when the season ends.

## 1. The Space Math

Two measurements decide everything:

- **Outer footprint plus buffer.** Plan the pool's outer dimensions plus a 0.5–0.8 m access buffer on all sides for walking, valve access, pump operation, and skimming. A Ø 3 m pool wants roughly a 4 × 4 m zone; a Ø 2 m pool fits a 3 × 3 m corner.
- **Level surface.** The base must sit flat — paving, concrete, or a level, mown lawn. Uneven pavers and slopes stress the walls and create trip hazards at the rim.

If the measured space is tighter than the buffer allows, step down a size: the [space planning guide](/guides/how-much-space-do-you-need-for-an-inflatable-pool) covers the arithmetic, and [patio placement](/guides/placing-an-inflatable-pool-on-a-patio) covers hard-surface setup in detail.

## 3. The Balcony Question — Answered Carefully

> **Critical:** water weighs about 1 tonne per cubic metre. A compact pool can easily add 1.5–4 t of load.

Standard residential balconies, rooftop terraces, and suspended decks are designed for people and furniture — not concentrated water loads. **Never place a filled pool on an elevated structure without written sign-off from a licensed structural engineer** who has reviewed the actual load rating. Ground-level patios and yards are the safe default; for them, a protective ground cloth under the hull keeps the base safe from patio grit.

## 4. Controlled Drainage in Close Quarters

Dumping several hundred litres toward a shared fence is how neighbors become enemies:

- **Use the drain valve, not a tip-over.** The hose-compatible valve releases water in a controlled flow you can route.
- **Direct water to approved points** — a storm drain, a cleanout, or distributed across your own garden beds.
- **Stop chemical treatment 48–72 hours before a drain** if the water is headed for planting areas.
- **Drain gradually** if your yard drains slowly; ponding against shared walls causes damage and disputes.

## 5. Privacy, Noise, and HOA Rules

- **Check HOA and building rules first** for temporary seasonal structures, placement near property lines, and any water-feature restrictions.
- **Screen sightlines** with freestanding privacy screens, planters, or shade sails where neighbors overlook the pool.
- **Place pumps on a vibration-dampening mat** so mechanical hum does not echo between close walls.
- **Keep fill levels at the recommended line** to minimize splash-over onto adjoining patios.
